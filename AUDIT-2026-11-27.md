## The independent source check

The cadence audits before #342 sampled ten cells of every tenth unit, and arithmetic cannot see a `standalone` cell. So this review ran one mechanical check over the whole corpus that depends on neither.

**Method.** For every unit row, the multiset of its cell values must be found on **one printed line** of its source: a line of the PDF character layer on the cited page (−2 to +4 pages; whole document as a fallback for re-anchored rows), or one `<tr>` of the HTML source. Tokens were built from `pdfplumber` characters (the May statement's undecoded font and the rotated Census pages needed their own handling); footnote superscripts were dropped by font size. A wrong value passes only if it happens to equal another number on the same printed line.

| Family | Rows checked | Rows found on one printed line | Not matched |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| treasury-mts | 6,565 | 6,562 | **3 — real, see F1** |
| census-p60 | 1,291 | 1,290 | 1 — printed `Z` transcribed as `0`, a documented convention; the other value is on the line |
| omb | 1,491 | 1,491 | — |
| bls-cpi | 329 | 329 | — |
| sec-10k | 245 | 245 | — |
| fec | 399 | 347 | 52 capstone rows that combine several printed blocks by design; every one of their values is printed in the document |

**What it does not prove.** It checks values, not their position inside the row, not that the row sits under the right label, and not that a printed number is absent from the unit. Those remain the job of the declared arithmetic and of the cadence audits. Labels were not checked corpus-wide at all.

The three failures were then confirmed on 3.0× page renders, read by eye.

## Findings

### F1 — High: three June cells do not match the print

| Unit | Cell | Unit has | Source prints | Role |
|---|---|---:|---:|---|
| `#318` `2026-06-table6-schedule-e-guaranteed` | `r39c6` Net Activity, Guaranteed Loan Financing — close of month (p32) | 12652 | **12,562** | `total` |
| `#323` `2026-06-table7-outlays-leg-def` | `r2c11` Judicial Branch — Comparable Period Prior F.Y. (p34) | 7632 | **7,134** | `standalone` |
| `#327` `2026-06-table7-outlays-ssa-independents` | `r2c11` Federal Disability Insurance Trust Fund (Off-Budget) — Comparable Period Prior F.Y. (p35) | 120164 | **120,134** | `standalone` |

All three shipped on 2026-07-19 in batches `#311–#320` and `#321–#330`, when the cadence audit looked at the tenth unit only.

`#323` and `#327` are plain misreads in cells no relation touches. The repair is to set the printed value; nothing else moves.

`#318` is the more instructive one. The unit's 12,652 is what **both** roll-forwards compute (14,894 − 2,242 and 18,056 − 5,404), so the unit reconciles exactly — with a number the page does not print. The same column of the same page already carries the corpus's one adjudicated source discrepancy, two rows up (`r37c6`: prints −53,892, both identities give −53,982, kept as printed with `tol="90"`). So p32 column 6 has two cells that each miss their identities by exactly 90, and the corpus treats them in opposite ways. By the corpus's own rule (cells are printed values; a total that does not foot is a finding), `r39c6` should read `12562` and its two relations should carry the same documented `tol="90"` as `r37`. That is an adjudication, not a mechanical fix, so it was left for a decision.

This is the defect class arithmetic is blind to by construction: a value "corrected" to make the sums close passes every gate.

**Update 2026-11-27 — repaired.** All three cells now hold the printed values, and the two `r39c6` relations in `#318` carry the documented `tol="90"`. The repair turned up one more fact: the July statement opens both lines at the computed values (12,652 and −53,982), and each June printed value is the computed one with two adjacent digits swapped. So both are errors in the June print, kept as printed. Record: the *Directed repair* entry in [AUDITS.md](AUDITS.md).

### F2 — Medium: the README promises more than the arithmetic delivers

The README says each table "must reconcile exactly", that "correctness is derived, not trusted", and that every total is targeted and every leaf feeds a relation. All true — for the 80.3% of cells that are `leaf` or `total`. The other **11,172 cells (19.7%)** take part in no relation, 37 units have no relation at all, and for those "GREEN" means "valid JSON with labels". Two of the three F1 errors were in exactly such cells.

A reader of a public dataset will take the README at its word. It should state the split in numbers and say what covers the unverified part (the different-agent audits, and now this row-level check). Suggested wording is a decision for the owner; the facts to put in it are in the table above.

**Update 2026-11-27 — reworded.** The README's opening now limits the "derived, not trusted" claim to cells that feed a printed total, and a new section, *What is verified, and how*, gives the split by family, the 37 units with no relation, what the audits and the row-level check cover, and a list of known gaps (F3, F5, F8).

### F4 — Low: the tolerance rule has documented exceptions the README does not mention

The README says a tolerance is only legal when the source's own rounding note is quoted. Of 3,410 non-zero `sum` tolerances, 3,338 are within the strict rounding bound (half a unit of the last printed digit per source and for the total). 70 exceed even one whole unit per source:

- **64 Census relations** (tolerance 5–50 on two to five sources): the printed counts carry four significant digits (131,400; 84,330), so sums drift by tens. Adjudicated source-side on 2026-08-18. (**Correction 2026-11-27:** the range is 5–70, not 5–50; the largest is in `2023-income-a6-people-2022`.)
- **2 relations in `#318`** at `tol="90"`: the source discrepancy described in F1 — explicitly *not* rounding, and the `why` says so. (**Update 2026-11-27:** four after the F1 repair — `r37c6` and `r39c6`, two each. The totals above become 3,412 and 72.)
- **4 relations in `bls-cpi/relative-importance-2024-education-other`** at 0.008–0.009 on three or four three-decimal sources, about four times what display rounding allows. Both the CPI-U and CPI-W columns show it, which suggests a component the table does not print rather than a typo, but the `why` attributes it to rounding. Worth one look.

Also, 497 `why` texts give the rounding note without quotation marks; all are verbatim or close paraphrases of a printed note.

Every non-zero tolerance in the corpus equals its observed delta exactly. That is the house rule, and it means a tolerance can never fail at ship time — the plausibility bound in `RUNBOOK-auditor.md` is the only thing standing between a typo and a fitted tolerance. It held everywhere checked.

**Update 2026-11-27 — stated in the README.** Its *Tolerances* subsection lists the 72 relations above one unit per source by group and says which are not rounding. The BLS item is listed there as unresolved.
